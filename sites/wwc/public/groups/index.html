How a team reaches the quarter-finals

1st in group → straight to the quarter-finals. Skips a knockout game entirely.
2nd and 3rd → qualification play-off, single elimination, cross-group.
4th → eliminated. There is no classification round and no consolation bracket.

Two things follow, and they are why every group game matters: winning your group is worth an entire knockout game, and finishing fourth ends your tournament on day four. A group winner cannot meet the runner-up from its own group before the final.

If teams finish level

Teams are ranked on classification points — two for a win, one for a loss. That is not the system most US fans expect: a 3–0 team has six, an 0–3 team has three, and nobody finishes on zero.

1. Classification points
2. If teams are level, only the games between those teams count — they are re-ranked as a mini-table: record, then points difference, then points scored, all within that group of teams
3. Still level → points difference across all group games, then points scored across all group games
4. Still level → FIBA world ranking

Step 2 is the one worth understanding, because it is not the same as "head-to-head". With three teams tied, beating one of them does not put you above them — what counts is your record and margin across all the games among the tied teams. A team can win its head-to-head meeting and still finish below the team it beat. And each time the procedure separates one team out, it starts again from the top for whoever is still tied.
